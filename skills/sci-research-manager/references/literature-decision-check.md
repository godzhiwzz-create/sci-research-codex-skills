# Literature in research decisions

Use when proposing a new method for decision, judging novelty or feasibility, deciding whether work is worth pursuing/testing, changing a signal's role, or planning a costly experiment expansion. Apply even when the idea came from discussion or a negative result rather than a paper. Pure concept naming/explanation without a decision or investment question remains ordinary discussion. Do not impose this check on ordinary reading, known-paper intake, bug fixes, or execution within an unchanged approved protocol.

## Start from the decision

State the current question, proposed action, allowed information, and what a result would change. For method proposals, separate the observed signal, its interpretation, the action it enables, and the target benefit. Do not choose the manuscript stance for the user.

Reuse the relevant project question route, source notes, and experiment card first. Before downloading or preparing assets solely to justify a proposal, screen its closest mechanism and direct predecessors; reverse this order only when an asset fact is needed to state the comparison. A tag search returning nothing may mean poor indexing, so check known method names, synonyms, and linked source IDs before concluding the local library lacks a source. Then check current primary sources for novelty-sensitive claims. Do not reread the entire library or require bibliographic registration before discussing a publicly identifiable paper.

Use `sci-literature-manager` for discovery and `sci-paper-reader` for mechanisms, controls, and scope. They are bounded skill calls, not automatic subagent delegation. Give each the decision question; a paper list or abstract summary is not a completed decision handback.

## Check the nearest competing work

Compare only sources that can affect the decision; use no fixed paper, query, time, or token quota. Start with the exact mechanism, its synonyms, and direct citation neighbors. Expand only to resolve a named uncertainty. Stop when the newest expansion does not alter the closest overlap, consequential protocol difference, or evidence-required next action; record the unresolved coverage instead of continuing by inertia. Read decisive methods/ablations/protocol sections in the primary source and record which portions were actually read. Abstract-only candidates remain screening evidence.

Use a compact table in the existing brief or discussion:

| Source/version/location | Mechanism and available information | Overlap and consequential difference | Decision/design affected |
|---|---|---|---|

Do not manufacture a gap. A missing benchmark, new name, component combination, or untested condition is not sufficient novelty. An information-permission difference is consequential only when it changes the feasible training/deployment inputs or the estimand under a fair data/compute/selection comparison; name the exact unavailable variable and required control. Conversely, conceptual similarity, a failed proxy, or a published negative recipe does not rule out every new intervention. Explain the substantive overlap, why any remaining difference would matter, and what result would distinguish it.

## Finish with a useful decision

Return these items concisely, without creating a second registry:

- What to reuse, cancel, revise, or retain because of the literature; if it changes nothing, say so.
- The unresolved project-specific question, separate from what the paper established.
- The cheapest next action and how positive, negative, or ambiguous outcomes change the plan. If every outcome merely triggers another parameter sweep, revise the question.
- Search date, mechanisms/terms and source families checked, decisive locations, and material unread/missing evidence. This is bounded coverage, not proof of exhaustive novelty.

Reuse the project's decision vocabulary. `reference_only` can mean a valuable reproduction or baseline without originality. `needs_literature` must name the exact unresolved source/claim and a bounded next retrieval action; do not leave it as an endless survey request.

Refresh a saved comparison only when the method, information boundary, decisive source version, named uncertainty, or relevant prior art changes; ordinary repeated execution does not repeat the survey.

For an interactive direction discussion, return this comparison in the conversation and discuss the unresolved scientific choice before opening an execution workflow. Do not turn each possible idea into a new intake, remote inventory or experiment. A decision is accepted for durable write or execution only when the user explicitly chooses it or a pre-authorized autonomous workflow crosses its predeclared gate; agent preference alone is not acceptance. In autonomous research already authorized by the user, record the next decision in the same working note and continue within scope; do not request renewed approval for routine steps.

## Before committing compute

For a novel-method experiment or costly expansion, make the decision check traceable in the existing brief/card before launching. This includes ideas not inspired by literature. A compact section is enough; do not require a new template collection or a second user approval for an already authorized action. A benchmark reproduction can proceed as such even without novelty.

Prefer existing artifacts or a no-training check when they answer the unresolved question. If they cannot distinguish the explanations, a small controlled training intervention can be the cheapest valid check. Do not require a complete observational causal explanation before allowing method experiments, and do not treat an oracle diagnostic as deployable benefit.

Freeze the consequential comparison: information access, candidate/sample/compute budgets, selection rules, baseline strength, and the control needed for the stated question. Which quantities need matching depends on the estimand; do not mechanically require matching everything or condition away the intervention itself. Never use target-test outcomes to choose the new method or threshold.

## After results

Return to the same brief/card. Say which assumption changed, whether the result repeats a known phenomenon or adds a bounded finding, and which next action is now unnecessary. Preserve valid negative evidence at its tested scope. A resource decision to stop an exact recipe is not a proof that the whole mechanism is impossible.

During read-only discussion, keep this in the response or an authorized scratch artifact. At an accepted project decision, persist one compact note/brief using the existing schema and link it from the existing question route. Do not write headline numbers or transient research conclusions into this skill. Paper intake and exact LiteratureClaim promotion remain separately owned operations.
